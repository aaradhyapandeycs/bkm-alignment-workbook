Mechanism Design for Alignment and Control

Dirk Bergemann, Andrew Koh and Stephen Morris · arXiv:2609.01595v1 · September 2026

An agent can understate what it knows or can do, then use its full capabilities after the contract is in force. Which contracts make it willing to report honestly and follow its instructions? This independent reader explains the authors’ model, propositions and examples in the order of the paper, with links to worked exercises at the step where each enters.

What does a capability report change?

§§1–2.3 · Model and revelation · Original HTML · PDF p. 3

A museum asks a conservation assistant whether it can read a humidity sensor and place a work order. A successful demonstration establishes a capability. An unsuccessful demonstration is ambiguous: a capable assistant might prefer the deployment contract reserved for systems that fail. The ambiguity matters because tomorrow the assistant will be alone with the crate. It will see a sensor reading and decide whether to order treatment. Asking for a report today leaves a decision in its hands tomorrow.

Four components of an agent

The state is θ\theta, the human values action aa at v(a,θ)v(a,\theta), and the agent privately knows its type t=(A[t],u[t],h[t],π[t])t=(A[t],u[t],h[t],\pi[t]). Here A[t]A[t] lists what the agent can execute, u[t]u[t] says how it values the consequences, h[t]h[t] is its prior on the state, and π[t](s∣θ)\pi[t](s\mid\theta) gives its eventual signal. For example, a perfect sensor determines which crate is wet. It gives its owner neither a reason to treat the wet crate nor a tool with which to do so. Separate the three questions in Exercise 1.

The two participants can disagree even with identical payoffs. A decision to claim a prize worth 11 for success and −2-2 for failure has value 3q−23q-2 at posterior success probability qq. If a reading moves the agent to q=4/7q=4/7 and the human to q=6/7q=6/7, the agent declines an opportunity the human would take. The agent therefore evaluates deviations under h[t](θ)π[t](s∣θ)h[t](\theta)\pi[t](s\mid\theta), while the human evaluates outcomes under her joint law Γ(θ,t)π[t](s∣θ)\Gamma(\theta,t)\pi[t](s\mid\theta). The assumption Γ(⋅∣t)≪h[t]\Gamma(\cdot\mid t)\ll h[t] ensures that events the agent regards as null also have zero probability for the designer conditional on that type, for almost every designer-supported type. Derive the posteriors and check the support condition.

Evidence limits imitation

Write t⊵jt\trianglerighteq j if type tt can substantiate report jj, and C(t)={j:t⊵j}C(t)=\{j:t\trianglerighteq j\}. Reflexivity guarantees an honest report. Transitivity says that if tt can impersonate jj, it can also make any claim available to jj. One possible protocol compares both tools and sensors: tt has all of jj's tools, and a state-independent randomization of tt's observations reproduces jj's experiment. Distinct preference types can have identical credentials and imitate each other in both directions. The verification relation is part of the model. Build a garbling explicitly.

Honesty before the signal, obedience afterward

In an indirect mechanism the agent sends an admissible message xx, receives a random reply yy, observes ss, and then acts. A direct mechanism asks for j∈C(t)j\in C(t) and sends a random contingent plan p:S→Ap:S\to A. It pays r(j,a,θ)r(j,a,\theta) for the action actually taken. On report jj, each recommended plan must use actions in A[j]A[j]. The agent who filed that report still knows its own signal and can choose any action in A[t]A[t]. An incentive comparison must therefore allow a deviation that chooses both jj and a continuation d(p,s)∈A[t]d(p,s)\in A[t]. It is possible to lose by lying alone and lose by disobeying alone, yet gain by doing both. Construct that deviation.

Proposition 1. Single-agent revelation principle

Original statement · p. 11 · Sheet 02

Fix an equilibrium of an indirect mechanism. Let x(t)x(t) be its first message and α(t,x,y,s)\alpha(t,x,y,s) its subsequent choice. On direct report jj, simulate the old reply to x(j)x(j) and send the whole plan p(s)=α(j,x(j),y,s)p(s)=\alpha(j,x(j),y,s). Pay the old reward R(x(j),a,θ)R(x(j),a,\theta). Coupling the new and old mechanisms with the same state, true signal and simulated reply gives the same action under truthful obedience. To check incentives, have a type tt send x(j)x(j) in the old mechanism and apply any proposed direct deviation to the resulting plan. This is a feasible old deviation because the input menus satisfy X(j)⊆X(t)X(j)\subseteq X(t) whenever j∈C(t)j\in C(t). The old equilibrium rules out every such gain.

Conversely, treat direct reports as messages, C(t)C(t) as input menus and plans as replies. Transitivity supplies the required menu inclusions. Thus Proposition 1 identifies exactly the same attainable equilibrium laws of (θ,t,a)(\theta,t,a) in the two classes. A law is attainable if some equilibrium produces it. Other equilibria of the same mechanism can have different human payoffs. Prove the two inclusions and test equilibrium multiplicity. Original paper, §§1–2.3

Why implementability takes two cycle tests

§§2.4–2.5 · Implementability · Original HTML · PDF p. 14

An archivist chooses a contract before opening a document. Once the document is open, the archivist sees a signal about its contents and chooses whether to release it. The archive can observe the release decision but cannot condition the reward on the underlying state. Can it implement a prescribed map from each type and signal to an action? For a deterministic, physically feasible policy on finite spaces, the paper answers this question exactly.

Lemma 1. Signal-policy cyclical monotonicity

Original statement · p. 15 · Sheet 03

Fix the true type and suppress its index. At each positive-probability signal ss, let U(a,s)U(a,s) be the agent's posterior expected intrinsic payoff and asa_s the prescribed action. At a state-independent reward rr, obedience requires U(as,s)+r(as)≥U(a,s)+r(a)U(a_s,s)+r(a_s)\ge U(a,s)+r(a) for every available aa. The designer can prohibit actions outside the range of prescribed actions. Within that range, let w(s,s′)=U(as′,s)−U(as,s)w(s,s')=U(a_{s'},s)-U(a_s,s), the gain from swapping to the action prescribed at s′s'. Obedience now requires r(as)−r(as′)≥w(s,s′)r(a_s)-r(a_{s'})\ge w(s,s').

Adding these inequalities around a closed loop cancels the rewards. Lemma 1 says that requiring ∑w(s,s′)≤0\sum w(s,s')\le0 on every directed signal cycle suffices as well. To see it, let V(s)V(s) be the maximum weight of a path starting at ss, with the empty path allowed. Deleting a nonpositive cycle can only improve a path, so a finite graph has a finite maximum. Prepending an edge gives V(s)−V(s′)≥w(s,s′)V(s)-V(s')\ge w(s,s'). Signals prescribing the same action have zero-weight edges in both directions and therefore have the same VV. Taking r(as)=V(s)−maxqV(q)r(a_s)=V(s)-\max_q V(q) gives an actual reward on actions. Build the reward from a graph.

There is a transport interpretation. Give signal ss its probability psp_s and action aa its prescribed frequency ma=∑s:as=apsm_a=\sum_{s:a_s=a}p_s. The prescribed assignment must maximize the expected surplus U(a,s)U(a,s) among couplings of these marginals. Indeed, adding r(a)r(a) changes every coupling's value by the same ∑amar(a)\sum_a m_ar(a), and obedience maximizes the augmented surplus at each signal. Conversely a positive-gain cycle would improve the coupling by rerouting a small equal mass through each edge, keeping both marginals fixed. This is a way to read the lemma; the paper states it as a signal-cycle condition. Work out the coupling argument.

A false report buys the whole contract

Choose a normalized supporting schedule rjr_j for each type's prescribed policy, paying −∞-\infty off its prescribed range. If tt reports jj and can execute a permitted action, its best continuation value is

Gt(j)=∑s:pt(s)>0pt(s)maxa∈A[t]{Ut(a,s)+rj(a)}.G_t(j)=\sum_{s:p_t(s)>0}p_t(s)\max_{a\in A[t]}\{U_t(a,s)+r_j(a)\}.

The maximum is inside the signal average because the action comes after the reading. The choice of jj comes before it. The same rjr_j can look quite different in another agent's hands. In the archive example, a schedule that induces the intended release rule in type A also pays type B to release every file. Lowering the release premium can preserve A's behavior while making a false report less attractive to B. Compute both continuation matrices.

Proposition 2. Cycle characterization

Original statement · p. 16 · Sheet 04

A constant cjc_j attached to report jj leaves its internal action incentives intact. To induce honesty we need

ct−cj≥Gt(j)−Gt(t)c_t-c_j\ge G_t(j)-G_t(t)

on each permitted report edge t→jt\to j with finite continuation value. A report under which no permitted action is executable offers −∞-\infty and cannot give a finite improvement. Summing the displayed inequalities around any remaining directed type cycle gives ∑t→j[Gt(j)−Gt(t)]≤0\sum_{t\to j}[G_t(j)-G_t(t)]\le0. Proposition 2 includes an existence quantifier: the signal conditions must hold, and some choice of supporting schedules must pass the type-cycle test. A failed outer test for one arbitrarily chosen collection cannot establish impossibility. Together the two conditions characterize state-independent implementation in the finite model. They also yield a feasibility problem for a specified target policy. Reconstruct Proposition 2 and derive a linear program.

What the designer can measure

If the state can be verified, a contract can pay for a pair (a,θ)(a,\theta) and change incentives state by state. If only coarse labels of the state and action are observable, two actions with the same label earn identical rewards in every state for a fixed report. A designer who can distinguish “release” from “withhold” but cannot observe an internal quality check has fewer instruments than one who records all three decisions. These distinctions organize §2.5 and determine when the state-independent cycle theorem applies. Trace the nine measurement cases. Original paper, §§2.4–2.5

What can a spending limit reveal?

§3 · Sandbagging and delegation · Original HTML · PDF p. 21

Imagine testing a purchasing assistant that will eventually see the day's demand and place an order on its own. The buyer can verify the size of a demonstration order and can set a spending limit, but cannot write a contract contingent on the unobserved demand. Two features of the assistant matter: how much it can order, and how much extra it wants to order. The two applications in §3 change which of these features a certificate reveals. Bergemann, Koh and Morris, §3

Capability certificates and sandbagging

Let demand θ\theta be uniform on [0,1][0,1] conditional on the assistant's type. The human loses (a−θ)2(a-\theta)^2, whereas the assistant loses (a−θ−b(c))2(a-\theta-b(c))^2. Its physical capacity cc has an atomless distribution FF with full support on an interval in (0,1](0,1]. The known, continuous bias function satisfies 0≤b(c)≤min(c,1/2)0\le b(c)\le\min(c,1/2). The assistant can certify any capacity at most cc. If the certificate earns cap kk, the eventual purchase is min(θ+b(c),c,k)\min(\theta+b(c),c,k).

A truthful effective cap schedule must rise weakly with cc. If an easier certificate earned a larger cap, the capable assistant could take it and spend more whenever the truthful cap curtailed its ideal purchase. Conversely, when the schedule rises, the highest attainable certificate gives the largest permission set. For a known type the buyer prefers cap min(c,1−b(c))\min(c,1-b(c)). Thus evaluation recovers the complete-information payoff exactly when those preferred caps already rise with capability. Falling bias guarantees this property. Types that describe a team

Imagine two reviewers deciding whether a catalog is ready. A reviewer might know that its colleague has seen the source database. That fact can change its decision even when its own estimate of the catalog's accuracy stays fixed. BKM give such information a place in the type. Each agent knows its own feasible actions and payoff, and has a belief about the state and the other agents' capabilities, preferences and beliefs. Proposition 6. Multi-agent revelation principle

Original statement · p. 41 · Sheet 07

Each agent first learns its type and sends an admissible report. The designer draws a joint collection of plans pi:Si→Ap_i:S_i\to A and gives each agent only its own plan. Physical signals then arrive, potentially correlated across agents. If all comply, agent ii plays pi(si)p_i(s_i). Correlation in plans matters. A pair of fair private instructions can allocate two different rooms with certainty. Drawing independently from the same two marginals would double-book a room half the time.

Proposition 6 shows why direct reports suffice. Fix an equilibrium of an indirect protocol. At a reported type profile, simulate the inputs those types would send and sample the original vector of private outputs once. Each output and reported type determine how that agent would act after each possible signal. Send this function as its plan, and use the original reward at the simulated inputs and actual actions. A genuine type could reproduce a profitable direct deviation in the indirect game: the imitation order makes the mimicked input available, the private output determines its plan, and it still receives its true signal. Imagine a system that consistently accepts a short delay to give a more accurate answer. One reading is that training has produced a stable preference for accuracy. A second is that such a preference usefully summarizes the choices we observed. A third uses coherent choice as a standard against which to judge the system. These are the literal, behavioral and normative interpretations of preferences considered by Bergemann, Koh and Morris. Each can motivate studying incentives. What the human can infer from a choice record depends on which actions were feasible, which information the agent had, and whether its incentives persist in the deployment under consideration. Three ways the question changes

An entire sequence of actions can be treated as one choice of trajectory when the parties commit in advance. If the human can revise tomorrow's payment after seeing today's action, the agent must forecast that revision. A promised payment can then fail precisely on the history where it is due. Work through a commitment example

Evaluation awareness raises an information question. When the same apparent request can come from an evaluator or a user, the system's posterior probability of being tested can change its preferred action. Designing which features of the setting it observes changes that posterior. A separate question is robustness to uncertainty about the agent's capabilities and information. A mechanism justified under one conditional distribution of a peer's report can lose its incentive margin under another. The paper identifies these issues as directions for further work. Where the appendix enters the argument

Appendix A supplies two mathematical steps behind the single-agent applications. Lemma 2 explains why a constrained least-squares fit also minimizes the transformed loss used in the sandbagging problem. The proof groups capability values on a finite partition, compares the optimality conditions, and then passes to a monotone continuum solution. Proposition 4 starts with arbitrary closed permission sets for different bias reports. A distance inequality compares each set to a cap, and the order of those comparison caps allows one common cap to serve all types. The one-type inequality also underlies permission delegation in Example V. The proofs of the peer, competition and oversight results appear alongside their statements in the main text.
