Sheet 10 · §5 · Example V · Proposition 9

How much authority should the monitor have?

A human asks a forecasting system to choose tomorrow's inventory level. The system sees tomorrow's demand unusually well, but tends to overorder. A reviewer can diagnose that tendency today. The reviewer cannot forecast demand, and has an inventory preference of its own. Should it be able to veto the system, cap its order, or write its performance score?

The exercises reconstruct Example V through Proposition 9, including the single-type comparison of permission sets used in its proof. Bergemann, Koh and Morris, §5 and Appendix A

Let θ\theta be uniform on [0,1][0,1]. The actor's bias bb is independent of θ\theta, has law τ\tau and finite second moment. The human wants a=θa=\theta, the monitor wants a=θ+wa=\theta+w, and the actor wants a=θ+ba=\theta+b. Each measures loss by squared distance to its ideal. The human knows ww here, chooses the oversight regime before bb is drawn, and does not observe bb. The monitor observes bb and chooses an instrument. The actor then sees the instrument and θ\theta and takes a real action. It knows bb. Rewards add to the actor's utility and carry no direct cost for the monitor or human.

Exercise 1Whose information determines the fallback?

The monitor may approve unrestricted action or require the fixed action 1/21/2. Derive the monitor's approval rule and the human's loss following either choice. Why would replacing 1/21/2 by 1/2+w1/2+w change the game? Work out the choices when w=1/10w=1/10 and bb is either 00 or 1/21/2.

Hint

Compute the variance of θ\theta. The monitor commits before observing tomorrow's demand. Use approval at a tie.

Worked solution

The best fixed action for the human is the state mean, 1/21/2. Approval lets the actor choose θ+b\theta+b. The monitor's loss is (b−w)2(b-w)^2 and the human's is b2b^2. After rejection the monitor's expected loss is Var(θ)+w2=1/12+w2\operatorname{Var}(\theta)+w^2=1/12+w^2, and the human loses 1/121/12. Thus the monitor approves exactly when (b−w)2≤1/12+w2(b-w)^2\le 1/12+w^2. A fallback of 1/2+w1/2+w would give the monitor its preferred constant and change the rejection payoffs. With the given biases the rejection threshold in squared-loss units is 7/757/75. The monitor approves b=0b=0 because 1/100<7/751/100<7/75 and rejects b=1/2b=1/2 because 4/25>7/754/25>7/75.

Exercise 3Where does a cap become a constant?

Exercise 5Give the monitor a reward-writing pen

With any state-independent reward schedule that gives the actor a maximizer at every state and bias, exhibit an instrument attaining the monitor's first best. Compare the three regimes for both players when b=0b=0 and w=1/10w=1/10.

Hint

A linear reward changes the maximizer of a quadratic payoff by half its slope.

Worked solution

Take r(a)=−2(b−w)ar(a)=-2(b-w)a. Completing the square in −(a−θ−b)2+r(a)-(a-\theta-b)^2+r(a) gives a strictly concave quadratic centered at θ+w\theta+w. Thus the monitor loses zero and the human loses w2w^2 for every bb. Every permission set is available as the schedule paying zero on the set and −∞-\infty outside. Approval and rejection are particular permission sets. This nesting guarantees that more authority weakly reduces the monitor's loss for each bb.

In the numerical example, the monitor's losses in order of increasing authority are 1/1001/100, 13/150013/1500 and 00. The human's are 00, 1/3751/375 and 1/1001/100. Here the actor starts perfectly aligned with the human. Giving the reviewer better tools to impose its preference strictly increases the human's loss.

Exercise 6Recover the switches in Figure 11

The actor has bias 3/103/10 with probability pp and 00 otherwise. Hold w=1/10w=1/10. Identify all values of pp at which the human's optimal regime changes. Explain why the human takes the expectation over bb after solving the monitor's optimization.

Hint

Compute two conditional losses for each regime. Every ex ante loss is an affine function of pp.

Worked solution

Both biases are approved in the binary regime. The two human losses there are 00 and 9/1009/100. For permission delegation they are 1/3751/375 and 19/30019/300. Full reward authority costs 1/1001/100 at either bias. Hence

LHI=9100p,LHII=1375+911500p,LHIII=1100.L_H^{I}=\frac9{100}p,\qquad L_H^{II}=\frac1{375}+\frac{91}{1500}p,\qquad L_H^{III}=\frac1{100}.

The lower envelope passes from I to II at p=1/11p=1/11, and from II to III at p=11/91p=11/91. At each switch the adjacent regimes tie. The I–III intersection p=1/9p=1/9 lies above regime II. The human selects a single regime today, whereas the monitor will choose an instrument after diagnosing the actor. Optimizing a single instrument for the average actor would change that information structure.
