Sheet 06 · §3 · Example II · Propositions 4–5

When a common cap beats a menu

A buyer is comparing two purchasing assistants. One consistently orders a little too much. The other is sometimes exactly right and sometimes orders substantially too much. If either assistant can buy anything, the buyer can compare their mean squared excess orders. If the buyer can also set a spending limit, will that comparison still rank the two systems correctly?

This sheet develops Example II, Propositions 4 and 5, and the cap comparison in Appendix A. Bergemann, Koh and Morris, §3 · Appendix A

Conditional on each bias b≥0b\ge0, demand θ\theta is uniform on [0,1][0,1]. The human wants action θ\theta and the agent wants θ+b\theta+b; each loses squared distance from its ideal. All types have the full action set ℝ\mathbb R and learn θ\theta perfectly after reporting their privately known bias. Each report is available to each type. The human chooses a menu of nonempty closed permission sets DrD_r, with measurable induced losses. Under a truthful menu a type minimizes its expected loss by choosing the set assigned to its own report. The bias distribution τ\tau represents the human's uncertainty prior to a report. Write m=𝔼bm=\mathbb E b, v=Var(b)v=\operatorname{Var}(b), and M=𝔼b2=m2+vM=\mathbb E b^2=m^2+v.

Exercise 1Two technologies, one choice of instrument

Technology A has constant bias 1/41/4. Technology B draws either 00 or 2/4\sqrt{2}/4 with equal probability. Compare their full-discretion losses. Now impose a common cap q=3/4q=3/4. Which technology does the buyer prefer?

Hint

For b≤q≤1b\le q\le1, the cap binds when θ>q−b\theta>q-b. Split the buyer's loss integral at that threshold.

Worked solution

Full discretion gives action θ+b\theta+b and expected loss M=1/16M=1/16 under either technology. At cap qq and bias bb, expected loss equals

(q−b)b2+∫q−b1(q−θ)2dθ=qb2+(1−q)33−2b33.(q-b)b^2+\int_{q-b}^1(q-\theta)^2d\theta =qb^2+\frac{(1-q)^3}{3}-\frac{2b^3}{3}.

The respective third moments are 1/641/64 and 2/64\sqrt2/64. Thus B has lower capped loss by (2−1)/96(\sqrt2-1)/96. Equal second moments can produce equal control instruments and unequal welfare. Exercise 5 will show that 3/43/4 is in fact optimal among all truthful delegation menus for both.

Exercise 2What does a permission set tell us through its distances?

For a nonempty closed set DD, put dD(x)=infa∈D∣x−a∣d_D(x)=\inf_{a\in D}|x-a|. Let AD(t)A_D(t) and HD(t)H_D(t) be the agent's and human's expected losses when the actual bias is tt and the agent chooses a nearest point aD(θ,t)a_D(\theta,t) to θ+t\theta+t. Hint

The function dD2d_D^2 is locally Lipschitz. At every point with a unique nearest point, its derivative is twice the displacement from that point.

Worked solution

As θ\theta ranges uniformly from zero to one, the agent's ideal traverses [t,t+1][t,t+1] with unit density. Hence

AD(t)=∫tt+1dD(x)2dx,AD′(t)=dD(t+1)2−dD(t)2.A_D(t)=\int_t^{t+1}d_D(x)^2\,dx,\qquad A_D'(t)=d_D(t+1)^2-d_D(t)^2.

Lb′(q)={2q−1,q≤b,b2−(1−q)2,b<q<b+1,0,q≥b+1.L_b'(q)= \begin{cases} 2q-1,&q\le b,\\ b^2-(1-q)^2,&b<q<b+1,\\ 0,&q\ge b+1 . \end{cases}

Exercise 6Does making the technology more interpretable always help?
