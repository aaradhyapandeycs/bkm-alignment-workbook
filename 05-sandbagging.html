Sandbagging and the shape of a spending limit

A purchasing assistant can demonstrate that it can place an order worth 80 credits. An assistant that stops at 60 might have reached its limit, or it might simply have stopped early. Suppose the buyer publishes a rule assigning a spending limit to each demonstration. Which rules make it worthwhile to demonstrate one's full capability?

This sheet reconstructs Example I, Proposition 3, and Appendix A's Lemma 2. The proof of continuum ironing below uses level sets. Bergemann, Koh and Morris, §3 · Appendix A

The buyer wants expenditure aa close to demand θ\theta and loses (a−θ)2(a-\theta)^2. A type with capacity cc loses (a−θ−b(c))2(a-\theta-b(c))^2. The buyer knows the continuous function bb and the atomless full-support distribution FF of cc on [c‾,c‾]⊂(0,1][\underline c,\overline c]\subset(0,1]. Assume 0≤b(c)≤min(c,1/2)0\le b(c)\le\min(c,1/2). Conditional on each cc, demand is uniform on [0,1][0,1]. The assistant submits a capacity certificate before it learns demand. It then sees demand perfectly and chooses an action under the awarded upper cap. Demand cannot enter the contract. This application optimizes within the class of upper-cap policies.

Exercise 1Which direction can a certificate lie?

Type cc can certify any r≤cr\le c in the type space and physically perform any a≤ca\le c. A report rr earns permission a≤k(r)a\le k(r). Derive the action after a report. Explain why nominal caps can be clipped at their reported capacities. For two possible certificates 0.80.8 and 11, take k(0.8)=0.8k(0.8)=0.8, k(1)=0.6k(1)=0.6, and b(1)=0.2b(1)=0.2. Find a state in which the larger type benefits from underperforming. Characterize all truthful normalized schedules 0≤k(c)≤c0\le k(c)\le c.

Hint

Permission and physical capability each impose an upper bound. A larger interval of available actions has option value even when the cap binds only occasionally.

Worked solution

Projection of the ideal action gives a=min(θ+b(c),c,k(r))a=\min(\theta+b(c),c,k(r)). Replacing k(r)k(r) by min(k(r),r)\min(k(r),r) preserves what type rr does under truth and weakly reduces every imitator's options. In the example, at θ=0.7\theta=0.7 the ideal is 0.90.9. Reporting 11 gives squared loss 0.090.09 and reporting 0.80.8 gives 0.010.01. The improvement persists on an interval, hence is profitable before demand is known.

An increasing schedule (weakly throughout this sheet) gives the truthful type the largest feasible cap. Conversely, if r<cr<c and k(r)>k(c)k(r)>k(c), then k(c)<k(r)≤r<c≤1k(c)<k(r)\le r<c\le1. States with θ+b(c)>k(c)\theta+b(c)>k(c) have positive probability and some strictly better action is available under report rr in every such state. Truthfulness is therefore equivalent to monotonicity. Negative caps can be raised to zero throughout a monotone schedule, improving the buyer's payoff wherever the change occurs.

Hint

If two neighboring fitted values violate the order, first try pooling them and minimizing their sum of squared residuals.

Worked solution

The targets are (0.0144,0.16,0.0324)(0.0144,0.16,0.0324) and their floors are (0.04,0.01,0)(0.04,0.01,0). Exercise 5Can costly evaluations be dispensed with when bias rises?

Suppose bb is weakly increasing. First optimize a single nominal cap aa, with effective cap min(c,a)\min(c,a). Treat both a binding optimum and the case when the physical frontier alone suffices. Then prove that even a menu contingent on the certificate cannot improve the payoff.

Hint

Differentiate the common-cap payoff using the types with c>ac>a. At a binding optimum compare a competing monotone schedule by the tangent inequality for VbV_b.

Worked solution

If c‾≤1−b(c‾)\overline c\le1-b(\overline c) then every type has c≤1−b(c)c\le1-b(c). Giving full physical discretion achieves the pointwise optimum, so one can take a=c‾a=\overline c. In the other case c‾>1/2\overline c>1/2. An optimum in [0,c‾][0,\overline c] exists by continuity. Caps below 1/21/2 with binding types can be improved. For 1/2≤a<c‾1/2\le a<\overline c, atomlessness and bounded derivatives give the marginal payoff

Pr(c>a)[(1−a)2−𝔼[b(c)2∣c>a]].\Pr(c>a)\left[(1-a)^2-\mathbb E\big[b(c)^2\mid c>a\big]\right].

The bracket is nonnegative at 1/21/2 and tends to (1−c‾)2−b(c‾)2<0(1-\overline c)^2-b(\overline c)^2<0 at the upper endpoint. It decreases strictly by monotonicity of bb and is continuous by atomlessness. Thus its sole root a∗∈[1/2,c‾)a^*\in[1/2,\overline c) satisfies

a∗=1−𝔼[b(c)2∣c>a∗].a^*=1-\sqrt{\mathbb E[b(c)^2\mid c>a^*]}.

For an arbitrary feasible schedule h(c)h(c), concavity bounds its gain over k∗(c)=min(c,a∗)k^*(c)=\min(c,a^*) by ∫Vb(c)′(k∗(c))(h(c)−k∗(c))dF\int V'_{b(c)}(k^*(c))(h(c)-k^*(c))\,dF. Below the threshold, h(c)−c≤0h(c)-c\le0 and b(c)≤𝔼[b2∣c>a∗]=1−a∗≤1−cb(c)\le\sqrt{\mathbb E[b^2\mid c>a^*]}=1-a^*\le1-c, so Vb(c)′(c)≥0V'_{b(c)}(c)\ge0. Above the threshold, the derivative is a decreasing function of cc with conditional mean zero, while h(c)−a∗h(c)-a^* increases.
